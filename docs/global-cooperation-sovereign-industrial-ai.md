Accelerated investment should fund redesign and testing where needed, rather than assume that existing dataspace implementations already satisfy AI-first requirements."

<h2 id="abstract">Complete abstract</h2>
<p>Industrial AI needs infrastructure that connects evidence across companies and jurisdictions at operational speed. European dataspaces are a first step in participant-controlled cooperation, not a demonstrated infrastructure foundation for IPCEI-AI. Dataspace concepts and policy precede today&#39;s LLM, frontier and agentic AI wave; existing governance and exchange mechanisms do not establish fitness for high throughput, low latency and large data volumes. We argue for <strong>fast-track modernisation</strong> that meets workload-specific performance requirements without relaxing access control, verifiability or participant authority. Substantial engineering and validation remain.</p>
<p>Palantir&#39;s operational ontology provides a demanding architectural reference for connecting data, meaning, logic and governed actions. Our proposed counter-model extends that operational ambition across independently governed implementations, with credible provider choice. Its foundation is <strong>access-controlled, verifiable evidence graphs built on open international standards and industry-agreed semantics</strong>. These graphs connect decision-relevant claims and provenance while bulk data can remain in specialist systems.</p>
<p>We combine primary-source review, platform economics, comparable architecture patterns and a proposed industrial experiment. The resulting agenda joins efficient data movement, task-specific graph views, scoped authorization and maintained evidence status. Europe can lead this modernisation through global coordination with US innovators, partners across Asia-Pacific including China, and other participant-controlled ecosystems. Joint vocabulary governance, implementation profiles and reciprocal testing must turn shared principles into working interoperability. This is a collaborative blueprint for global digital sovereignty and a competitive multipolar economy, not a claim of production readiness. Performance, assurance, resilience and economic gains remain outcomes to demonstrate together. Accelerated investment should fund redesign and testing where needed, rather than assume that existing dataspace implementations already satisfy AI-first requirements.</p>
