It distinguishes improving behavior from controlling external effects, and examines how current identity, authority and evidence can inform an independent runtime decision. The selection is a dated, purposive qualitative study; Spherity’s graph-based runtime path is research and integration design. A separate strategic outlook examines eIDAS 2.0, the proposed European Business Wallet Regulation and potential European–US trust partnerships."

<section class="safety-proposition" aria-labelledby="the-2027-proposition"><h2 id="the-2027-proposition">The 2027 Proposition</h2><p>As AI agents move from giving advice to taking consequential actions, we expect safety to become a defining condition for deployment in 2027. <h2 id="introduction">1. Introduction</h2>

<p>A safety claim about an AI model answers a different question from a decision to let an AI system change a record, place an order or propose a maintenance deviation.</p>

<p>Model evaluation can establish that a specified failure occurred under a reproducible test. The same evaluation leaves open what a deployed system can reach through its tools, who delegated authority to use those tools, and whether a counterparty has current evidence for the exact action. A network boundary can block an unauthorized connection. A protected controller can reject a request that violates a rule. When a supplier, registry, laboratory or customer supplies evidence for that rule, the relying party must additionally decide what the evidence means, who issued it, and whether the issuer had authority for that scope. [<a href="#ref-1" aria-label="Reference 1">1</a>, <a href="#ref-2" aria-label="Reference 2">2</a>, <a href="#ref-3" aria-label="Reference 3">3</a>]</p>

<p>This paper asks three questions. First, which disciplines address these distinct failure modes, and where do they overlap? Second, what do the documented activities of ten companies reveal about the distribution of those disciplines? Third, how can evidence from several organizations support a runtime decision without giving an AI agent control of its own authorization?</p>

<p>The first contribution is a two-axis model that places five disciplines across four domains of work and treats cryptographic verifiability as a common evidence mechanism. The second is a case-coded comparison of ten companies, accompanied by a separate landscape of research organizations. The third is an analysis of registry-backed authority and semantic assurance graphs at the point of execution, illustrated by a hypothetical aerospace maintenance workflow. This extends the German technology outlook's treatment of complementary capabilities and cross-company trust to a global AI safety landscape. [<a href="#ref-4" aria-label="Reference 4">4</a>, <a href="#ref-5" aria-label="Reference 5">5</a>, <a href="#ref-6" aria-label="Reference 6">6</a>]</p>

<h3 id="from-evidence-to-a-decision">From evidence to a decision</h3>

<p>An audit trail created after an effect helps investigators reconstruct what happened. A verified record can also change the decision before the effect occurs. If a mandated approval has been withdrawn, an independent controller can stop a pending action even when the model recommends proceeding. If a valid credential refers to a superseded component or a different operating configuration, the same controller can require a fresh review. The value comes from linking the evidence to a control that can intervene, with timing and enforcement designed around the consequence. [<a href="#ref-2" aria-label="Reference 2">2</a>, <a href="#ref-5" aria-label="Reference 5">5</a>, <a href="#ref-7" aria-label="Reference 7">7</a>, <a href="#ref-8" aria-label="Reference 8">8</a>]</p>

<h2 id="scope-and-selection-method">2. Scope and selection method</h2>

<p>This is a comparative landscape study. Its unit of analysis is an evidenced safety activity, grouped by organization for presentation.</p>

<h3 id="case-selection">Case selection</h3>

<p>AI safety includes work that reduces harmful or unreliable behavior, misuse and loss of control, or the consequences of AI-mediated actions. The company cases were selected purposively for the importance of the risk addressed, specificity of public evidence, operational reach and contribution to distinct disciplines. Frontier developers qualify through deployment at scale. Large divisions are grouped with their parent companies. Spherity is the single size exception, selected for its specialization in authority and evidence across organizations. The research organizations were selected for institutional remit, named public outputs, relevance beyond their own deployments and coverage of different research functions and jurisdictions. [<a href="#ref-1" aria-label="Reference 1">1</a>, <a href="#ref-3" aria-label="Reference 3">3</a>, <a href="#ref-4" aria-label="Reference 4">4</a>]</p>

<h3 id="evidence-and-activity-coding">Evidence and activity coding</h3>

<p>Public frameworks, research, technical documentation, independent assessments, and government or counterparty records were reviewed to 8 November 2026. Evidence is described by what it establishes. A framework documents a process, a service document describes an available capability within its stated scope, a report can establish a test or incident, and a manuscript can establish a proposed design. Spherity's company-supplied statements are identified separately.</p>

<p>For each company, one primary quadrant captures the selected contribution most clearly supported by the cited material. Additional quadrants are coded where a specific adjacent activity is documented. Placement concerns the function of the named activity, including the stage at which it acts and whether its immediate object is behavior or an external effect. The code carries an evidence label where the selected activity is a research or integration design. Unmarked cells indicate the scope of the reviewed evidence. The coding is qualitative, with no composite score or weighting.</p>

<h3 id="interpretive-limits">Interpretive limits</h3>

<p>The study compares visible activities under different disclosure regimes. An evaluation covers its test conditions, and products or governance programs can span multiple quadrants. <h3 id="runtime-verification-and-revocation">Runtime verification and revocation</h3>

<h4 id="an-action-decision-uses-the-current-evidence-path">An action decision uses the current evidence path</h4>

<p>At the point of commitment, an independent controller admits the exact action only if it is mediated, covered by a current delegation, and supported by the required evidence for the subject, configuration and conditions. It resolves only the policy's required dependency path. A valid signature on a document from an issuer does not establish the issuer's right to grant the requested authority. Each required edge has to satisfy its own issuer, scope and status rules. [<a href="#ref-2" aria-label="Reference 2">2</a>, <a href="#ref-5" aria-label="Reference 5">5</a>, <a href="#ref-6" aria-label="Reference 6">6</a>, <a href="#ref-12" aria-label="Reference 12">12</a>]</p>

<p>The controller can verify stable evidence at ingestion or update, then re-evaluate affected dependencies, current mandate status and the exact action at commitment within a defined freshness budget. Shared limits must be reserved consistently across agents. An audit receipt can bind the canonical action, policy version, evidence snapshots and outcome. Revocation blocks future or pending effects where the enforcement path can still intervene. Completed effects require a separately authorized remedy or escalation. [<a href="#ref-5" aria-label="Reference 5">5</a>, <a href="#ref-7" aria-label="Reference 7">7</a>, <a href="#ref-23" aria-label="Reference 23">23</a>]</p>

<p>The graph manuscript's threat model shows why stronger credentials should be applied where they can change the outcome. If substituting one accepted authority credential would alone change the decision, the cost of that feasible substitution places an upper bound on the modeled decision assurance. A weaker content dependency or a joint attack can also determine that bound. This directs review toward dependencies on which the decision actually turns. [<a href="#ref-6" aria-label="Reference 6">6</a>]</p>

<p>Where applicable, Article 14(4)(e) of the EU AI Act addresses human intervention and interruption to a safe state in high-risk AI systems. NIST's AI Risk Management Framework calls for assigned responsibility and mechanisms for disengagement or deactivation. Section <a href="#spherity-trust-infrastructure">6</a> applies these principles to the proposed kill switch. [<a href="#ref-3" aria-label="Reference 3">3</a>, <a href="#ref-24" aria-label="Reference 24">24</a>]</p>

<h4 id="qualified-authority-and-semantic-interoperability">Qualified authority and semantic interoperability</h4>

<p>Under eIDAS, qualified electronic attestations of attributes and in-scope public-sector attestations have specified legal effects. Company data and powers of representation are among the authentic-source categories in Annex VI. The current EUDI Wallet issuance references for the categories studied use specified SD-JWT VC and mdoc profiles. A graph-proof profile eligible for qualified issuance is a standards proposal in the manuscript. The European Business Wallet proposal's treatment of linked attestations and mandates provides the policy context. Other jurisdictions require their own issuer and legal-recognition rules. [<a href="#ref-6" aria-label="Reference 6">6</a>, <a href="#ref-19" aria-label="Reference 19">19</a>, <a href="#ref-20" aria-label="Reference 20">20</a>, <a href="#ref-21" aria-label="Reference 21">21</a>, <a href="#ref-25" aria-label="Reference 25">25</a>]</p>

<p>Spherity participates in the W3C Verifiable Credentials Working Group. Spherity's Konstantin Tsabolov contributed to recognition vocabulary work in the Credentials Community Group and is credited for use-case groundwork in the Recognized Entities v1.0 Working Draft. This work matters for assurance graphs because the relying party needs machine-readable terms for which issuer is recognized, by whom, for which actions and under what scope. The Working Draft and the graph-proof proposal have different status from the W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0 Recommendation. [<a href="#ref-6" aria-label="Reference 6">6</a>, <a href="#ref-12" aria-label="Reference 12">12</a>, <a href="#ref-91" aria-label="Reference 91">91</a>, <a href="#ref-92" aria-label="Reference 92">92</a>, <a href="#ref-93" aria-label="Reference 93">93</a>]</p>

<p>Semantic interoperability requires shared identifiers, versioned terms and explicit mappings. A requirement labelled “approved test” must bind to the relevant test method, part revision and issuer authorization across the parties that exchange it. A vocabulary makes the relationship interpretable. A proof binds the issuer to the stated relationship. The resource owner's policy determines whether that evidence satisfies the requirement. [<a href="#ref-6" aria-label="Reference 6">6</a>, <a href="#ref-8" aria-label="Reference 8">8</a>, <a href="#ref-13" aria-label="Reference 13">13</a>, <a href="#ref-17" aria-label="Reference 17">17</a>]</p>

<h4 id="openshell-integration-work">OpenShell integration work</h4>

<p>Spherity's published technical assessment describes how Business Wallets, legal representation, scoped mandates and verifiable evidence could integrate with NVIDIA's OpenShell and Sentry. OpenShell supplies an isolated execution environment and enforceable resource policy. The proposed Spherity layer supplies verification of the principal, delegation, evidence path and current status for a particular action. NVIDIA's development documentation for Agent Policy Fabric describes an external policy-information interface and signed policy provenance. It offers an example integration point for a verifier that returns a scoped result. The protected endpoint remains responsible for final enforcement and fallback. This is a documented integration design. [<a href="#ref-5" aria-label="Reference 5">5</a>, <a href="#ref-7" aria-label="Reference 7">7</a>, <a href="#ref-11" aria-label="Reference 11">11</a>, <a href="#ref-46" aria-label="Reference 46">46</a>, <a href="#ref-50" aria-label="Reference 50">50</a>, <a href="#ref-94" aria-label="Reference 94">94</a>]</p>

<p>The closest adjacent activities help define the specialization. Google AP2 addresses verifiable mandates and payment evidence for checkout. Microsoft Entra supplies agent identity and conditional access. NVIDIA provides runtime isolation and policy provenance. Spherity's distinctive combination in the cases reviewed is legal authority rooted in business registers, semantic evidence spanning multiple organizations and a design for current-status verification and revocation before a protected action. The evidence for each element has its own maturity. [<a href="#ref-6" aria-label="Reference 6">6</a>, <a href="#ref-7" aria-label="Reference 7">7</a>, <a href="#ref-31" aria-label="Reference 31">31</a>, <a href="#ref-35" aria-label="Reference 35">35</a>, <a href="#ref-46" aria-label="Reference 46">46</a>, <a href="#ref-82" aria-label="Reference 82">82</a>, <a href="#ref-90" aria-label="Reference 90">90</a>, <a href="#ref-93" aria-label="Reference 93">93</a>]</p>

<h3 id="spherity-ai-safety-research">Spherity AI safety research</h3>

<p>Spherity's open AI safety research specifies the controls that connect verifiable authority and evidence to a decision at the resource boundary. The papers cover the initial decision, continued operation as conditions change, and the withdrawal of authority. [<a href="#ref-5" aria-label="Reference 5">5</a>, <a href="#ref-7" aria-label="Reference 7">7</a>, <a href="#ref-81" aria-label="Reference 81">81</a>]</p>

<h4 id="protected-execution-and-continuing-assurance">Protected execution and continuing assurance</h4>

<p>Resource-Bounded Program Equilibria and Protected Execution separates an agent's incentives from its authority to act. The paper derives a conditional unilateral-deviation criterion and uses omission counterexamples to test a deterministic controller. The controller binds the exact action to current delegation and evidence, prevents replay and enforces a shared budget across concurrent requests. [<a href="#ref-95" aria-label="Reference 95">95</a>]</p>

<p>Risk-Bounded Runtime Assurance requires both a current mandate and an assurance lease for the exact proposal. A persistent risk account carries pending effects and shared dependencies across worker replacement. The paper derives a first-harm bound under explicit calibration, model-coverage and enforcement assumptions, and evaluates the controller across 432 synthetic scenarios. The controller can therefore evaluate a continuation without resetting the risk budget. [<a href="#ref-96" aria-label="Reference 96">96</a>]</p>

<h4 id="private-verification-and-revocation-across-organizations">Private verification and revocation across organizations</h4>

<p>Private Verification of Action Eligibility proposes verifying the claims and prohibitions that apply to a particular action with limited disclosure. Its W3C Verifiable Credentials and ODRL profile gives counterparties common terms for eligibility. The evaluation checks nine invariants in a finite controller model. The separate revocation series examines withdrawal races at commitment, status freshness and unresolved remote effects. Its two-operation baseline explores 6,651 reachable states. [<a href="#ref-63" aria-label="Reference 63">63</a>, <a href="#ref-97" aria-label="Reference 97">97</a>]</p>

<h4 id="configuration-specific-evidence-for-physical-ai">Configuration specific evidence for Physical AI</h4>

<p>PACE links safety claims and tests to the approved configuration and its dependencies. Governed Memory for Physical AI extends the same logic to an agent's use of prior experience. Its bounded protocol explores 3,192 reachable states and tests whether changed calibration, assessments or permissions can affect a later operation. Together these studies make the distinction between evidence that can be reused and evidence that permits an action testable. [<a href="#ref-8" aria-label="Reference 8">8</a>, <a href="#ref-98" aria-label="Reference 98">98</a>]</p>

<p>The evidence consists of conditional proofs, finite-state checks, counterexamples and synthetic tests. Field assurance requires separate validation of the deployed control path and its operating conditions. <h4 id="kill-switch-and-safe-resumption">Kill switch and safe resumption</h4>

<p>A kill switch must bring the affected workflow to a defined safe state. An independent local stop or validated fallback must remain available during model, graph, network or remote identity outages. For coordinated containment, verifiable graphs supply the applicable policy triggers, the affected agents and resources in the domain graph, and the pending effects in the episode graph. The decision engine checks evidence provenance, applicability and freshness, authenticates the remote operator or safety service, and verifies its current authority to intervene within that scope. At the resource boundary, the controller blocks new effects, suspends or revokes permissions, and cancels or hands off pending work where safe. It records enforcement and tracks unresolved remote effects until their outcomes are verified. The hold stays in force until recovery conditions are verified and resumption is separately authorized. [<a href="#ref-2" aria-label="Reference 2">2</a>, <a href="#ref-3" aria-label="Reference 3">3</a>, <a href="#ref-5" aria-label="Reference 5">5</a>, <a href="#ref-7" aria-label="Reference 7">7</a>, <a href="#ref-15" aria-label="Reference 15">15</a>, <a href="#ref-63" aria-label="Reference 63">63</a>]</p>

<p>An engineering agent proposes extending the inspection interval for an aircraft component. The example concerns a maintenance approval workflow and illustrates the graph manuscript's design. <p>The supervisor loads the base clause and any authorized deviation. It verifies issuer, version, validity and status, and checks the deviation's link to the base clause and its applicability to the installed configuration. The controller compares the proposed interval with the effective limit and routes the request to the maintenance approval workflow, retains the existing interval, or escalates. A signed test report for a superseded revision can remain authentic while failing the applicability check. The receipt records the policy and source snapshots used for the decision. [<a href="#ref-5" aria-label="Reference 5">5</a>, <a href="#ref-6" aria-label="Reference 6">6</a>, <a href="#ref-8" aria-label="Reference 8">8</a>]</p>

<p>Fast local protective functions use their separately validated controls. Stable graph material can be verified on load or update. The approval path rechecks relevant status, exact action and scope at commitment according to its freshness policy. If a required approval is revoked, no longer current or inconsistent, the supervisor retains the approved state or escalates. Engineering approval and operational responsibility remain with the appropriate qualified functions. [<a href="#ref-6" aria-label="Reference 6">6</a>, <a href="#ref-7" aria-label="Reference 7">7</a>, <a href="#ref-15" aria-label="Reference 15">15</a>, <a href="#ref-16" aria-label="Reference 16">16</a>]</p>

<h2 id="evaluation-implications-and-limitations">8. Evaluation implications and limitations</h2>

<h3 id="how-the-proposed-integration-can-be-evaluated">How the proposed integration can be evaluated</h3>

<p>An end-to-end test should measure the rate of unauthorized effects, false blocks on valid requests, time from revocation to enforcement at the last preventable step, and whether an independent reviewer can reconstruct the decision. Measure latency under cold starts, warm caches, update delays and concurrent actions, including the tail of the distribution. Test a signed but inapplicable report, a changed context or stale status snapshot, a withdrawn mandate between approval and commitment, a network outage, and two requests that together exceed a shared limit. Define admissible fallback behavior for each case. [<a href="#ref-5" aria-label="Reference 5">5</a>, <a href="#ref-6" aria-label="Reference 6">6</a>, <a href="#ref-7" aria-label="Reference 7">7</a>] Exercise the kill switch during outages and during races with commitment. Test forged or replayed remote stop commands, and verify that the hold persists until authorized resumption.</p>

<p>The manuscript's benchmark studies particular prototype verification paths, including repeated checks after graph aggregation. Its results depend on the profile, workload and implementation, and exclude parts of the complete decision path. The aerospace case provides a testable architecture. Operational use would require validation of the whole control path, including status distribution, failure recovery, configuration binding and sector-specific approval. [<a href="#ref-6" aria-label="Reference 6">6</a>, <a href="#ref-15" aria-label="Reference 15">15</a>]</p>

<h3 id="limits-of-the-company-comparison">Limits of the company comparison</h3>

<p>Public documentation varies by organization, jurisdiction and risk domain. The company matrix is a qualitative coding of selected activities as of the evidence cut-off. Comparative incident rates and control effectiveness would require separate study designs. The Spherity case combines company records, partner evidence, public research and an unpublished manuscript. The production wallet claim is company-reported. The graph-based runtime path is a research and integration design whose operational properties require separate evidence.</p>

<h3 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h3>

<p>The comparison distinguishes interventions that improve behavior, validate and monitor systems, limit reachability, control effects and establish which evidence a controller can rely on. Their combination matters when an AI system can affect resources owned by another organization. The party controlling the effect must be able to check the relevant requirements and authority at the last point where it can still intervene. Registry-backed identity and verifiable assurance graphs give that party a way to evaluate those inputs across organizational boundaries. The proposed architecture can be judged by whether its authority and evidence checks change outcomes under realistic failure and revocation tests.</p>

<section aria-labelledby="verifiable-ai-safety-european-trust"><h2 id="verifiable-ai-safety-european-trust">Verifiable AI safety: eIDAS 2.0 and European Business Wallets</h2>
<p class="safety-edition">Strategic outlook · HTML commentary added 8 November 2026; separate from the edition 2.6 PDF. The European Business Wallet Regulation remains a proposal at this research cutoff.</p>
<p>Within the ten companies reviewed in this paper, Spherity is the only company identified with a dedicated specialization combining register-backed business identity, delegated authority and semantically interoperable assurance graphs for verifiable AI safety. Its high-assurance cryptographic verifiability approach builds on eIDAS 2.0 trust infrastructure and anticipates the proposed European Business Wallet Regulation. Operating identity infrastructure provides the foundation; graph-based runtime assurance remains a research and integration design.</p>
<p>Compliance matters because a valid signature alone does not establish who may authorize an AI agent to act for a company. eIDAS provides a legal framework for trusted identity and attestations; qualifying attestations can carry recognized legal effects. Verifiable AI safety also needs scoped mandates, current status, revocation and evidence that a relying party can check. These mechanisms help connect technical proof with accountable business authority. Qualified status depends on the issuer and applicable attestation requirements; wallet compliance alone does not certify AI safety.</p>
<p>European providers embedded in company registers, qualified trust services and cross-border wallet pilots are especially well equipped to connect these legal and technical layers. That advantage comes from institutional integration and regulatory experience. We expect US AI companies serving European regulated workflows to rely increasingly on European partnerships for verifiable identity, authority and compliance evidence, while contributing models and platforms. This is a strategic expectation, not a universal legal requirement or an observed market outcome.</p>
