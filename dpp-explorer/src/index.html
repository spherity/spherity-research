What is a knowledge graph?

A knowledge graph organises information as things and the relationships between them. In this atlas, each labelled card or dot is a node: a regulation, standard, initiative or other research record. Each line is an edge: a specific relationship recorded from a source. Following a connection helps you see how one development relates to another, rather than reading each item in isolation.

SEMANTICS, VALIDATION & REUSE

Read the markers as independent attributes

An open-world graph can use strict validation rules and a licensed vocabulary at the same time. The badges describe specific, sourced properties; they do not rate a standard as good or bad.

∞ Open world
Missing information need not be false. The model leaves room for additional facts and vocabulary. It does not mean that all data is public, freely reusable or automatically accepted.
□ Closed checks / lists
A particular validation profile restricts permitted properties or values. This is more precise than labelling an entire ecosystem “closed-world”. Using SHACL or JSON Schema alone does not establish that every shape is closed.
§ Use licence
The vocabulary has publisher-specific use conditions to check. This does not mean every use costs money. Open licences also impose terms; licensing and semantic assumptions are different questions.

Parallel initiatives

Our interpretation is that local and sector-specific initiatives have developed in parallel, with global convergence still emerging. Separate clusters can reflect this fragmentation, but a missing line alone does not prove incompatibility.

02 / NEXT 6–24 MONTHS

Test the connections

Global trade, wider US participation and demand for interoperable DPPs create incentives to connect ecosystems. The paper separates demonstrated technical exchange from institutional recognition and tests four possible scenarios. Its observation checkpoints track evidence, without assuming universal mutual acceptance.

Foundations & what “open-world” means

Open-world semantics leave room for additional facts: information missing from the graph is unknown rather than automatically false. Access control determines who may see evidence; verifiability helps check its origin and integrity.

  • UNTP technical specifications — a developing framework for interoperable product and trade evidence.
  • W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 and the OWL 2 Primer — verifiable claims and open-world semantics.
  • DHS update on US Customs and Border Protection interoperability tests — evidence of US participation in standards-based trade infrastructure, not a nationwide DPP mandate.

These sources explain the foundations. Future connections require documented evidence; the frozen release remains unchanged.

READ THIS MAP AS RESEARCH

Every connection has a context.

The frozen baseline, release 0.1.0, contains 203 entities, 224 sourced assertions and 112 source records. The current graph includes dated evidence additions checked on 25 October 2026. The searchable records exclude geography and vocabulary scaffolding.

Scope is explicit

EU joins represent instrument scope, not completed national legal reviews. Global initiatives are shown separately. A German ecosystem association does not turn an international standard into national law.

Evidence remains qualified

Sources, dates, conditions and access limits travel with each record. Human review is pending. Reported exchanges are attributed; no universal interoperability score is calculated.

A reproducible snapshot

Research release 0.2.0 reconciles the immutable baseline, seven retained website batches and dated manuscript evidence. Corrections and superseded claims remain in its change history. Downloads retain IDs, sources, qualifications and update provenance. Changing a filter does not change the evidence. The snapshot is frozen for reproducibility; its claims remain machine-assisted and await qualified human review.
