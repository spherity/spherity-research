      As AI agents move from giving advice to taking consequential actions, we expect safety to become a defining condition for deployment in 2027. Improving model reliability, validating and monitoring systems, isolating workloads, and enforcing deterministic controls for safe intervention must work together. \begin{abstract}
As AI systems acquire tools and delegated authority, improving their behavior and controlling their effects become distinct safety tasks. This paper develops a four-part framework that separates design and qualification from live operation, and model or system behavior from external actions. It applies the framework to a purposive selection of ten companies and examines the complementary work of ten research organizations. Public evidence shows different concentrations in frontier evaluation, application monitoring, and execution controls. Cryptographic verifiability can connect these activities by preserving the provenance, meaning and status of evidence, while each relying party remains responsible for deciding whether that evidence authorizes an action.

Spherity is the sole small-company selection. Its specialization combines registry-anchored organizational authority and semantic assurance graphs with proposed runtime checks and revocation. An aerospace maintenance example illustrates how a supervisor can evaluate sector, company and use-case requirements against the exact action. The analysis distinguishes operating trust infrastructure, documented tests and proposed integration. The selections describe evidenced contributions within a defined sample.
\end{abstract}
