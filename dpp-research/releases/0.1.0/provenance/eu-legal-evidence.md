# EU, EEA and UK DPP legal evidence

Research cut-off and access date: **23 October 2026**. Primary-source evidence notes for subsequent knowledge-graph and world-map scoping. Each section distinguishes legislation, future obligations, non-binding plans and operational developments. Search results sometimes returned original legislation rather than corrected/consolidated text; corrections are explicitly flagged below. Some EUR-Lex pages blocked direct opening; searchable indexed legislative text was available. This is a research evidence ledger, not a completeness certification of every national implementing measure.

## 1. ESPR is a framework, not an immediate passport obligation for all products

- **Instrument:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, dated 13 June 2024; entered into force 18 July 2024. Binding EU framework.
- **Pinpoints:** Articles 1, 4, 7–15, 18, 78; Article 78 adds battery-identifier registration to Batteries Regulation Article 77.
- It enables product-specific ecodesign and DPP requirements through delegated acts. The legal scope excludes several categories, including food/feed and medicinal products. Product-specific acts determine the applicable information requirements and operational details.
- For the graph, distinguish `establishesFramework`, `empowersDelegatedAct`, `amends` and `requiresPassportFor`. Do not derive immediate universal applicability from framework entry into force.
- [EUR-Lex consolidated ESPR, current version shown as 28 June 2024](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1781/2024-06-28/eng).
- [Commission FAQ, questions on applicability and exemptions](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport/explore-our-faqs_en): explicitly states there is no general DPP obligation under ESPR; the process is product by product. It also distinguishes adoption targets from legally binding compliance dates.

## 2. Working-plan dates are adoption targets

- **Status:** indicative Commission implementation timetable, not enacted product compliance deadlines.
- Commission timeline retrieved at the cut-off: iron/steel delegated act targeted Q4 2026; textiles, aluminium and tyres Q3–Q4 2027; furniture 2028; mattresses and recycled-content act 2029. Energy-related products have a 2026–2029 range. Adoption normally precedes compliance by a transition period.
- No adopted iron/steel product-specific DPP delegated act was identified in this research. Treat it as a targeted act until an Official Journal instrument is linked.
- The same Commission page has inconsistent DPP service-provider targets (Q2 and Q3 2027). Preserve this ambiguity; do not convert either into a legal deadline.
- [Commission DPP timeline, explicitly labelled indicative and subject to publication](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport_en).

## 3. Registry implementing law has been adopted

- **Instrument:** Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778, dated **16 July 2026**, published **17 July 2026**.
- **Pinpoint:** Article 1; recital 3 describes architecture.
- Article 1 expressly covers ESPR products, Article 77 batteries, Article 76 construction products, Article 19 toys, Article 21 detergents/end-user surfactants, and future EU laws requiring registry registration.
- Registry architecture includes user interface/API, verification, identification/authorisation, unique registration identifiers, identifier/commodity-code storage, semantic repository, logging, and service-provider references. This is a legally specified coordination layer for decentralised product information.
- [Official Journal implementing regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32026R1778).
- Research limitation: exact commencement article was not extracted. Do not infer its legal entry date solely from the launch announcement below.

## 4. Registry is operational, with a testing environment

- **Event:** Commission announced launch **20 July 2026**, including a testing environment and implementation support.
- This supersedes earlier descriptions of the registry as merely planned for July 2026. The news item says registration uses a secure interface or API, with optional electronic proof of registration and a semantic repository. Full product information remains decentralised.
- The Commission also states that references to harmonised parts of six DPP standards were already published; two remaining standards are shown for November 2026 on its indicative timeline. Exact standard references and Official Journal decisions need to be separate graph entities, not inferred from the announcement.
- [Commission: The Digital Product Passport Registry is now live, 20 July 2026](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/digital-product-passport-registry-now-live-2026-07-20_en).
- Classification: `operational infrastructure` plus `testing environment available`; this does not make all product-sector passport obligations applicable in July 2026.

## 5. Battery passport: exact scope and fixed date

- **Instrument:** Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, dated 12 July 2023; current consolidated text retrieved is **13 August 2026** and incorporates Regulations 2024/1781, 2025/1561 and 2026/1738.
- **Pinpoints:** Articles 77–78, Annex XIII; Article 48 for distinct due-diligence timing.
- **18 February 2027:** each LMT battery, each industrial battery **greater than 2 kWh**, and each electric-vehicle battery placed on the market or put into service must have a battery passport. The threshold applies to the industrial category; do not attach it to LMT/EV batteries or substitute “all batteries.”
- Information includes model-level and individual-battery information, with public, authority/notified-body and legitimate-interest access tiers. A passport is not wholly public.
- Battery due diligence was postponed to **18 August 2027**; this does **not** postpone the passport date.
- [Current consolidated Batteries Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/ojn).

## 6. Battery implementation guidance is evolving and non-binding

- **Event:** Commission published updated guidance on **21 August 2026**.
- It maps **71 data points** across EV, LMT and industrial-battery categories, distinguishing mandatory, optional, conditional, and fields not required to be completed/displayed in February 2027.
- It does not create new requirements and expressly disclaims authoritative interpretation or representation of the Commission’s official position. It is useful for a data-requirement graph but must be linked as guidance, not legislation.
- [Commission guidance announcement, 21 August 2026](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/guidance-support-preparations-digital-batteries-passport-2026-08-21_en).
- The Commission’s general DPP timeline still places the battery-access-rights implementing act in Q4 2026; no adopted act was identified here. Verify at manuscript freeze.

## 7. Construction products: enacted framework, conditional DPP start

- **Instrument:** Regulation (EU) 2024/3110, dated 27 November 2024; general application **8 January 2026**, subject to detailed transitional rules.
- **Pinpoints:** Articles 22(7), 75–80, 95(9), 96.
- Commission delegated act must establish the construction DPP system. Article 80 makes the system operational six months after that act enters into force; mandatory provision under Article 22(7) begins **18 months after that act enters into force**. Product-family transition also matters under Article 95(9).
- No universal January 2026 or 2027 construction-passport mandate can be inferred from this regulation alone. The Commission currently targets the relevant delegated act for Q2 2027; that is planning information.
- **Graph distinction:** construction-product DPP versus building logbook/building passport. The statute here concerns products.
- [Construction Products Regulation, Articles 22 and 75–80](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32024R3110).

## 8. Toys are adopted law, not still a proposal

- **Instrument:** Regulation (EU) **2025/2509**, dated **26 November 2025**.
- **Pinpoints:** Articles 19–22, particularly registry Article 22.
- Establishes toy DPPs and use of the ESPR registry for product/operator identifiers; customs-related commodity-code information is included. The legal text states registry communication does not prove substantive compliance.
- [Toy Safety Regulation, official text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32025R2509).
- **Entry into force:** **1 January 2026**. **General application:** **1 August 2030**. The Commission says all toys placed on the EU market must have safety/compliance passports under the new rules.
- [Commission confirmation, 23 December 2025](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/stronger-toy-safety-rules-enter-force-2025-12-23_en).
- A Council “adoption” announcement from October 2025 was its first-reading position, before Parliament approval; use the final regulation for adoption status.

## 9. Detergents are also adopted law

- **Instrument:** Regulation (EU) **2026/405**, signed **11 February 2026**.
- **Pinpoints:** Articles 21–25; Annex VI.
- Article 21 requires a passport before placing a **detergent or end-user surfactant** on the market; do not overgeneralise to every surfactant in an intermediate supply chain. Normally model-level, with links to other required passports and a single passport where other Union law also requires one. Article 21(10) requires an implementing act specifying technical details.
- [Detergents and Surfactants Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32026R0405).
- **Published:** 2 March 2026. **In force:** 22 March 2026. **Applies:** **23 September 2029**.
- [ECHA official overview and dates](https://echa.europa.eu/understanding-the-regulation-on-detergents-and-surfactants).
- Voluntary digital labelling is distinct from the mandatory DPP obligation.

## 10. New 2026 vehicle law adds a further statutory passport

- **Instrument:** Regulation (EU) **2026/1738**, dated **8 July 2026**, published **24 July 2026**, in force **13 August 2026**. General application **1 September 2028**.
- **Pinpoints:** Articles 2, 13, 59.
- **Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport from 1 September 2032**, with implementing rules due **14 August 2030**. Article 13 expressly calls for alignment/interoperability and, where possible, integration with battery, Euro 7 environmental and ESPR passports; already accessible information should not be duplicated.
- Scope must retain Article 2 exceptions: principally M1/N1; heavy-duty and trailer categories are expressly excluded from Article 13, and other exclusions apply. Do not label this “all vehicles.”
- [Vehicle circularity regulation, Article 13 and scope](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32026R1738).
- [Parliament official summary confirming adoption and publication](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1910752).

## 11. CRMA permanent magnets: a narrower digital-information requirement

- **Instrument:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1252; **pinpoints:** Article 28(1)–(10), Article 34.
- Listed products containing specified permanent-magnet types require a label and unique-identifier-linked data carrier; information includes composition/location and removal instructions. If another law requires a product passport, Article 28(6) places that information in that passport.
- Main label/data-carrier timing is **two years after the relevant implementing act enters into force**, not a universal fixed 2027 passport date. Article 28(10) gives a separate 24 May 2029 provision for MRI devices, motor vehicles and type-approved category-L light transport; scope exceptions remain important.
- Treat as `passport-related digital information`, with conditional `includedInPassport` linkage, rather than a blanket DPP mandate on all critical raw materials.
- [CRMA consolidated text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02024R1252-20240503).
- Implementing-act adoption/completion was not established in this research; the statutory deadline is not evidence that an act was adopted.

## 12. CRMA corrigendum changes dates

- **Correction:** Official Journal **2024/90330**, **3 June 2024**.
- Changes the Article 28(2) implementing-act deadline from **24 November 2026 to 24 November 2025**; also corrects several other deadlines.
- [Official CRMA corrigendum](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AL_202490330).
- Graph implication: preserve version and corrigendum edges. Original-Act and search snippets may expose superseded dates, even on official sites.

## 13. PPWR should not become a universal packaging-DPP marker

- **Instrument:** Regulation (EU) 2025/40; generally applicable from **12 August 2026** (Article 71).
- **Pinpoints:** Article 12(2)–(6), recitals 69–70.
- Reusable packaging has a QR/standardised-open-data-carrier requirement from **12 February 2029 or 30 months after the relevant implementing act enters into force, whichever is later**, subject to open-loop exceptions. Article 12(5) requires the same carrier where Union law also requires digital information for the packaged product.
- Classify as digital labelling/traceability with passport reuse or integration, not a blanket standalone passport for every package. The Commission’s broad DPP policy web page includes PPWR in the ecosystem, but the operative provisions determine classification.
- [PPWR official text, Article 12](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32025R0040).
- Actual status and timing of the packaging-label implementing acts remains an evidence gap here.

## 14. European Business Wallet legislation remains a proposal

- **Proposal:** COM(2025)838, **19 November 2025**; procedure **2025/0358(COD)**.
- Council negotiating position adopted **9 June 2026**. Parliament committee vote/negotiation decision **10 September 2026**, report tabled 23 September, negotiating decision endorsed in plenary **7 October 2026**.
- Latest primary procedural record retrieved supports an ongoing legislative file, not an adopted regulation. A separate secondary tracker claims a first negotiation on 14 October; do not use that date as verified primary evidence here.
- [Commission proposal publication](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/proposal-regulation-establishment-european-business-wallets).
- [Council negotiating position](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/09/european-business-wallets-council-adopts-negotiating-position/).
- [Parliament current procedural events; French version includes October events](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/fr/procedure-file?reference=2025%2F0358%28COD%29).
- Graph role: proposed identity/representation/document-exchange infrastructure, not itself a product passport law.

## 15. eIDAS is a separate enacted trust framework

- **Instrument:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 amending Regulation 910/2014.
- **Pinpoint:** amended Article 5a(1), (4)–(5).
- EUDI wallets are for natural **and legal** persons; support identification, attestations, signing/sealing and selective disclosure. Member-State provision deadline is tied to entry into force of the relevant implementing acts, not simply 24 months from adoption of the parent law.
- [eIDAS amendment](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1183/oj).
- [Commission EUDI legal/technical roadmap](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/sites/spaces/EUDIGITALIDENTITYWALLET/pages/915931903/The+legal+and+technical+road+to+EU+Digital+Identity+Wallets): describes end-2026 rollout expectation.
- Relationship to DPP: enabling identity/trust infrastructure. Neither eIDAS nor the Business Wallet proposal alone establishes a generic DPP obligation. Whether a given protocol/credential is legally required must be evidenced separately.

## 16. UK: split Great Britain and Northern Ireland

- **Official process:** UK digital product records (DPR) call for evidence published **27 July 2026**, closed **21 September 2026**.
- **Pinpoints:** Executive summary; Current regulatory frameworks / Current UK regulatory approach.
- Government explicitly states it has **not yet determined its Great Britain DPR approach**. Northern Ireland EU DPP requirements apply under the Windsor Framework; the page specifically identifies ESPR and Batteries Regulation application and sector-specific toy/construction developments.
- Distinguish GB domestic law from UK exporters’ exposure to EU market requirements. Do not colour the whole UK as either uniformly EU-mandatory or uniformly voluntary.
- [UK official DPR call for evidence](https://www.gov.uk/government/calls-for-evidence/call-for-evidence-digital-product-record-policy/call-for-evidence-digital-product-records-policy).
- Caution: the UK page loosely describes 2027 construction requirements. For the EU construction DPP start, use CPR Articles 22(7)/80 and actual delegated-act entry rather than this shorthand. Detailed NI treatment remains instrument-specific.

## 17. EEA relevance is not proof of national applicability

- Norway’s energy authority NVE states ESPR **has not yet been incorporated into the EEA Agreement or Norwegian law** on the page retrieved at the research cut-off.
- [NVE official ESPR status page, section “Status for ESPR i Norge”](https://www.nve.no/energi/virkemidler/oekodesign-og-energimerking-av-produkter/oekodesignforordning-for-baerekraftige-produkter-espr/).
- [Innovation Norway official EU-product-regulation overview](https://www.innovasjonnorge.no/en/artikkel/er-produktene-dine-omfattet-av-nye-eu-regelverk) also distinguishes incorporation assessment from exporters’ EU-market obligations.
- For publication, check each act in EEA-Lex and national law individually, including Iceland and Liechtenstein. Do not automatically extend EU map shading from the words “Text with EEA relevance.” Current authoritative incorporation records were not retrieved in this pass.

## Research-design consequences

1. Store **legal instrument**, **provision/obligation**, **product category**, **geographic applicability**, **actor**, **data/access requirement**, **implementing instrument**, **standard**, **event**, and **source/version** separately.
2. Keep `adoptedAt`, `publishedAt`, `enteredIntoForceAt`, `appliesFrom`, `adoptionTarget`, `conditionalTrigger`, `verifiedAt` and `sourceUpdatedAt` distinct.
3. Use explicit statuses: enacted framework; enacted future obligation; currently applicable obligation; implementing act pending/unverified; legislative proposal; non-binding guidance; operational infrastructure.
4. Add integration edges across vehicle/battery/ESPR passports, detergents’ single-passport clause, CRMA magnet information and PPWR shared carriers. These relationships are stronger evidence of convergence than broad claims that every jurisdiction has the same DPP.
5. Keep source conflicts visible, especially official explanatory pages versus operative legal provisions and original versus corrected texts.
6. Recheck open gaps at manuscript freeze: ESPR sector acts, battery access-rights act, service-provider act, construction system delegated act, CRMA implementing act, PPWR labelling acts, EEA incorporation, and Business Wallet negotiations.
